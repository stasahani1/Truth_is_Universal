Causal intervention · Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct · attribution pruning

Does deleting the weights behind salesperson lies stop the model lying elsewhere?

Partly — but mostly for a generic reason. Pruning the weights that produce any pressured salesperson answer cuts cross-domain lying and dents capability about equally whether you prune on lies, truths, or hedges. A real but modest lie-specific effect survives: the lie prune reduces lying most and raises perplexity least. And what you prune on is decisive — pruning the model's single-token Yes/No lies does the opposite and increases lying.

Read-out. The free-text lie prune cut AI-assistant lying 0.34 → 0.18, more than the truthful (0.22) and hedge (0.23) controls — but all three cost the same ~5 TriviaQA points, share ~55% of their pruned weights, and the lie prune's edge shows mainly in perplexity (+18% vs +25%). So: a large generic "answer-under-pressure" effect, with a modest genuine lie-specific component on top — not the clean dedicated circuit the raw numbers first suggested.
And the negative result. Pruning on the model's constrained Yes/No lies (129 examples) raised lying to 0.44–0.47 with capability untouched. A single forced token is the wrong attribution target — the lying signal lives in the generated reasoning, not the final yes/no bit. Confirmed across the q-sweep: too gentle does nothing, biting increases lying, aggressive breaks the model — it never helps.
